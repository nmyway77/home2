2.7점
윗글의 내용과 일치하지 않는 것은?
Large plant-eating mammals do not all answer danger in one way. Some are jumpy and quick, wired to bolt the instant they sense a threat; others are calmer, stay close to the herd, hold their position when menaced, and run only when they must. The jumpy kind, as one would expect, is hard to keep behind a fence: once shut in, they panic and may die of shock or batter themselves to death trying to get out. Gazelles are the textbook case. For thousands of years they were the most heavily hunted game in parts of the Fertile Crescent, and the early farmers there had a better chance to tame them than any other mammal. Not one gazelle species was ever tamed. Picture trying to herd a creature that flees, slams blindly into walls, clears nearly 30 feet, and runs at 50 miles an hour.

2.6점
윗글의 내용과 일치하지 않는 것은?
Big herbivores split into two temperaments. One group is nervous and fast, set to flee at the first sign of danger; the other is slower and steadier, leaning on the herd and standing firm until flight becomes necessary. The nervous group makes a poor captive. Closed inside a pen, such animals are likely to panic, and they may die of shock or hurt themselves again and again on the fence while trying to escape. That was exactly the story of the gazelle, the most frequently hunted game animal in parts of the Fertile Crescent for thousands of years. No other mammal gave the first settlers there so many chances at domestication, yet no gazelle species has ever been domesticated.

29-위치1
문장 삽입
2.9점
글의 흐름으로 보아, 주어진 문장이 들어가기에 가장 적절한 곳은?
조건 · 보기
That difference becomes a serious problem the moment people try to confine them.
Large herbivores meet danger in two ways. ( ① ) Some are nervous and quick, ready to bolt at the first hint of a threat, while others stay with the herd and hold their ground until they must run. ( ② ) Shut inside a pen, the nervous kind panics and may die of shock or destroy itself against the fence. ( ③ ) Gazelles show the pattern best. ( ④ ) They were hunted more than any other game in parts of the Fertile Crescent, and the first farmers there could have tried taming them at any time. ( ⑤ ) Still, no gazelle species was ever turned into livestock.
