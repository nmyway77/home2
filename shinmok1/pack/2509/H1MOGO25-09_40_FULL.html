Vision is influenced1 by our preconceptions2 about reality. In viewing3 a scene, we establish unconscious4 hierarchies that5 reflect our functional relationship to objects and our momentary6 priorities. For example, when visualizing7 a hammer in our mind's eye, we tend to “see” it in profile or at some other “ready for use” angle. One would probably not visualize a hammer as seen8 from the top so that the handle is hidden by the hammer's head. The functional relationship we have with objects creates9 visual expectations10 that interfere11 with our ability to see12 “like a camera.” The camera, like the human eye, sees only shapes13 and colors. It documents the world impartially14 through a lens that is similar15 to the eye. When we look at them carefully, photographs are often surprising16 because they don't interpret confusing17 details but simply serve them up to us with a mechanical indifference18. And because of19 their flatness20, photographs often contain areas that appear as unrecognizable21 colors and shapes.

본문 문장별 해석영어 한 문장 · 우리말 한 문장
1Vision is influenced by our preconceptions about reality.
시각은 현실에 대한 우리의 선입견에 의해 영향을 받는다.
2In viewing a scene, we establish unconscious hierarchies that reflect our functional relationship to objects and our momentary priorities.
한 장면을 볼 때, 우리는 사물과의 기능적 관계와 순간적인 우선순위를 반영하는 무의식적 위계를 확립한다.
3For example, when visualizing a hammer in our mind's eye, we tend to “see” it in profile or at some other “ready for use” angle.
예를 들어 마음의 눈으로 망치를 시각화할 때, 우리는 망치를 옆모습이나 다른 ‘사용 준비 완료’ 각도에서 ‘보는’ 경향이 있다.
4One would probably not visualize a hammer as seen from the top so that the handle is hidden by the hammer's head.
손잡이가 망치 머리에 가려지도록 위에서 본 망치를 시각화하지는 아마 않을 것이다.
5The functional relationship we have with objects creates visual expectations that interfere with our ability to see “like a camera.”
우리가 사물과 맺는 기능적 관계는 ‘카메라처럼’ 보는 우리의 능력을 방해하는 시각적 기대를 만들어 낸다.
6The camera, like the human eye, sees only shapes and colors.
카메라는 인간의 눈처럼 오직 형태와 색깔만 본다.
7It documents the world impartially through a lens that is similar to the eye.
그것은 눈과 비슷한 렌즈를 통해 세상을 공평하게 기록한다.
8When we look at them carefully, photographs are often surprising because they don't interpret confusing details but simply serve them up to us with a mechanical indifference.
주의 깊게 들여다보면 사진은 종종 놀라운데, 사진은 혼란스러운 세부를 해석하지 않고 기계적인 무관심으로 그것을 우리에게 그저 내놓기 때문이다.
9And because of their flatness, photographs often contain areas that appear as unrecognizable colors and shapes.
그리고 평면성 때문에 사진은 종종 알아보기 어려운 색과 형태로 보이는 영역을 포함한다.

복합 <보기> 판별 (A)~(D)
3.3점
다음 글에 대한 <보기>의 설명 중 틀린 것만을 있는 대로 고른 것은?
What we see is shaped by the ideas we already hold about the world. As we take in a scene, we sort it, without noticing, into an order that mirrors how we use things and what we happen to care about at that moment. Picture a hammer in your mind, for instance: you will most likely imagine it from the side, or in some other position in which it is ready to be picked up. Hardly anyone would imagine it from directly above, with the head blocking the handle from view. Our practical relationship with things sets up visual expectations, and these get in the way of seeing the world the way a camera does. A camera, just like the eye, registers nothing but shapes and colors. It records the world without taking sides, through a lens that works much as the eye does. Looked at closely, photographs often catch us off guard, since they make no attempt to explain puzzling details but simply hand them over to us with mechanical indifference. Moreover, (ⓐ) [because / because of] their flatness, photographs frequently contain patches that look like colors and shapes we cannot identify.
<보 기>
(A) The hammer is used as an example of how our purposes shape the way we picture an object.
(B) ‘because’ is the right choice for (ⓐ), since the words that follow explain the reason.
(C) The sentence ‘A camera, just like the eye, registers nothing but shapes and colors.’ means that a camera, like us, ranks what it sees by its usefulness.
(D) In the summary ‘Our expectations, unlike a camera, prevent us from seeing things impartially’, ‘from seeing’ is correctly used after ‘prevent us’.
①(B), (C)
②(A), (D)
③(A), (B)
④(C), (D)
⑤(B), (D)

①[A] argues that cameras see better than people, while [B] argues that microphones hear worse than people.
②In [B], the recording sounds like noise because the phone's microphone is of poor quality.
③[A] says photographs explain confusing details for us, and [B] says our ears record every sound equally.
④Both texts claim that machines can never capture what human senses capture.
⑤Both texts contrast a device that records everything equally with human senses that select according to what matters to us.

1. 📖 문제 해석주어진 문장(카메라는 눈처럼 형태와 색만 본다)이 들어갈 자리를 지시어 단서로 찾는다.
2. 📖 지문/근거 해석주어진 문장의 The camera는 앞에서 “like a camera”가 언급된 뒤에 와야 자연스럽고, 바로 뒤 It documents the world impartially의 It이 The camera를 받아야 한다. 두 조건이 만나는 곳이 ④.
3. 📖 조건/선지 해석①~③은 아직 카메라가 등장하기 전(선입견·망치 예시)이라 The camera가 갑작스럽다. ⑤는 It documents 뒤여서 It이 가리킬 대상이 앞에 없다.
4. ✦ 정답 도출 + 오답 함정⑤ [한 끗] 사진 이야기 앞이라 어울려 보이지만, 그러면 It documents the world의 It이 ‘기대’를 가리키게 되어 어긋난다. ③ ‘카메라처럼’이 나오기 전이라 like the human eye의 비교가 뜬금없다.
