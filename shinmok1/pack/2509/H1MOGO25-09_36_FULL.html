The desert tortoise has a simple1 solution for coping2 with Death Valley's extreme heat: It avoids it. The slow-moving creature hibernates3 during the winter and stays in its tunnel for much of the summer, meaning4 that it spends more than 90 percent of its life immobile5. In fact, the tortoise usually only surfaces6 after a good rain. Then, it gets to work. The tortoise stocks up on water by eating plants and digging7 holes to collect rain. But to stay supplied with water through its extended8 hibernation, the reptile relies on something else — its highly9 sophisticated10 bladder11. Unlike most animals, the tortoise's bladder acts12 as a holding tank, allowing13 it to reabsorb14 water back into its body. Incredibly, a desert tortoise can go a full year without taking15 in any freshwater16 at all. And because its bladder is so important to a tortoise's survival17, park rangers often remind visitors not to stop18 and help the slow-movers across the road. Tortoises become so terrified when people pick19 them up that they empty their bladders, losing20 their precious21 water reserves.

36-1
복합 <보기> 판별 (A)~(D)
3.3점
다음 글에 대한 <보기>의 설명 중 틀린 것만을 있는 대로 고른 것은?
The desert tortoise deals with the brutal heat of Death Valley in the simplest possible way: it stays out of it. This unhurried animal sleeps through the winter and remains in its burrow for most of the summer, so that well over nine-tenths of its life is spent without moving. As a rule, it comes to the surface only once a heavy rain has fallen. That is when it becomes busy. It builds up its water supply by feeding on plants and by scooping out hollows where rainwater can gather. To remain supplied through its long period of sleep, however, the reptile depends on a different resource — a remarkably advanced bladder. Whereas the bladder of most animals simply stores waste, the tortoise's bladder works as a reserve tank from which water can be taken back into the body. Astonishingly, a desert tortoise can last an entire year without drinking any fresh water. Since this organ matters so much to the animal's survival, park rangers frequently warn visitors against stopping to carry the slow creatures across the road. A tortoise that is lifted becomes (ⓐ) [so / such] frightened that it releases the contents of its bladder, and with them its hard-won store of water.
<보 기>
(A) The park rangers' warning is presented as an example showing how much the bladder matters to the tortoise.
(B) ‘such’ is the right choice for (ⓐ), because ‘frightened’ describes the tortoise.
(C) The sentence ‘the reptile depends on a different resource’ means that plants and rainwater alone keep the tortoise supplied during its long sleep.
(D) In the summary ‘Avoiding the heat and storing water in its bladder allow the tortoise to survive’, the verb ‘allow’ agrees with its subject.
①(A), (D)
②(B), (C)
③(B), (D)
④(A), (B), (C)
⑤(C), (D)

( ② ) Then, it gets to work. ( ③ ) The tortoise stocks up on water by eating plants and digging holes to collect rain. ( ④ ) But to stay supplied with water through its extended hibernation, the reptile relies on something else — its highly sophisticated bladder. ( ⑤ ) Unlike most animals, the tortoise's bladder acts as a holding tank, allowing it to reabsorb water back into its body.

36-8 정답 & 4단계 해설
정답 ②
OP0삽입문 = 원문 S3(In fact, … after a good rain) · 단서: 앞 immobile(움직이지 않음) → In fact 강화 · 뒤 Then, it gets to work
1. 📖 문제 해석주어진 문장(사실 거북은 보통 충분한 비가 온 뒤에만 밖으로 나온다)이 들어갈 자리를 연결어·지시어 단서로 찾는다.
2. 📖 지문/근거 해석In fact는 앞 내용을 강화한다 → ‘생의 90% 이상을 움직이지 않고 보낸다’를 받아 ‘사실 비 온 뒤에만 나온다’. 그리고 바로 뒤 Then, it gets to work의 Then은 ‘비가 와서 나온 때’를 가리켜야 한다. 두 단서가 만나는 곳이 ②.
3. 📖 조건/선지 해석① 자리는 아직 동면·굴 이야기가 나오기 전이라 In fact가 강화할 내용이 없다. ③ 이후는 이미 ‘활동을 시작’한 뒤라 ‘비 온 뒤에만 나온다’가 끼면 흐름이 되돌아간다.
4. ✦ 정답 도출 + 오답 함정③ [한 끗] Then 뒤에 두면 ‘활동 시작 → 사실 비 온 뒤에만 나온다’로 순서가 뒤집혀 Then이 가리킬 사건이 사라진다. ① It avoids it 뒤에 ‘비 온 뒤에만 나온다’를 붙이면 그럴듯하지만 immobile 문장과 Then 사이가 끊긴다.
