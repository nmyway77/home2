윗글의 내용과 일치하지 않는 것은?
How we feel over the long run is not simply a running total of pleasant and unpleasant moments. A good event lifts the mood and a bad one lowers it, yet neither pushes us onto a permanently new course. Human beings absorb news of both kinds and then drift back to the level of contentment that is normally theirs. Peaks and dips come and go, but time, like water settling to its own surface, pulls us toward that level: upward after a loss, downward after a gain. The glow of a first romance fades, and so does the misery of a break-up. Small children show the pattern most clearly: the toy they have been begging for convinces them of lifelong happiness, and the conviction lasts a few minutes before they, like the grown-ups around them, get used to it.

The same is true of the low points.
Happiness is not a running total of separate moments. ( ① ) Good news lifts us, but the lift does not hold; within days we settle back to the level of contentment that is usually ours. ( ② ) A loss drags us down for a while, and then we climb back to that same level. ( ③ ) This is why the glow of a first romance cools and the misery of a break-up cools with it. ( ④ ) Small children make the process obvious: the toy they begged for guarantees lifelong joy for roughly five minutes. ( ⑤ ) After that, they adapt, exactly as their parents do.
