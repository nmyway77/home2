Whether we feel happy or sad, content or discontent, is1 not determined2 merely by each individual successive3 moment of life experience — a good thing happens and I’m happy, a bad thing happens and I’m sad. While our experiences affect our mood, we are not blown4 in a completely new direction5 by each gust of wind. As humans, we adjust — to new information and events both good and bad — and return to our personal6 default7 level of well-being. There will be8 highs and lows, but over time, like water seeking9 its own level, we are pulled10 toward our baseline11 — back up12 after bad news and back down after good. The euphoria13 of first love fades14, and so does15 the despair of a break-up. This tendency is best seen16 with little kids and their toy joy: When they get what17 they’ve longed18 for, they believe they will be happy for the rest of their lives. And for the first few minutes19 of the rest of their lives, they are20. But then the kids — like adults — adapt21.
