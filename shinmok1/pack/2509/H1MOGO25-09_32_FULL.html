Although you may put off going1 to sleep in order to squeeze more activities into your day, eventually your need for sleep becomes overwhelming2 and you are forced3 to get some sleep. This daily drive4 for sleep appears to be due, in part, to a compound known as adenosine5. This natural chemical builds6 up in your blood as time awake increases7. While you sleep, your body breaks down the adenosine. Thus, this molecule may be what8 your body uses to keep track9 of lost sleep and to trigger sleep when needed10. An accumulation11 of adenosine and other factors might explain why, after several nights of less than optimal12 amounts of sleep, you build13 up a sleep debt14 that you must make up by sleeping15 longer16 than normal. Because of such built-in molecular17 feedback18, you can’t become accustomed19 to getting20 less sleep than your body needs. Eventually, a lack of sleep catches21 up with you.
