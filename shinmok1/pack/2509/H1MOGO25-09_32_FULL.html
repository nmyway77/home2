윗글의 내용과 일치하지 않는 것은?
You can delay bedtime to fit more into the day, but not indefinitely: sooner or later the urge to sleep grows too strong to resist. Part of that urge comes from adenosine, a chemical that collects in the blood the longer you stay awake and is cleared away while you sleep. The body may use it as a running record of missed sleep, a signal to switch sleep on when it is needed. That record explains why a run of short nights leaves a debt you must repay with extra sleep. The feedback is built into your chemistry, so you cannot train yourself to need less; sooner or later the shortage catches up with you.

윗글의 내용과 일치하지 않는 것은?
Postponing sleep works only for a while; in the end the need for sleep overwhelms every other plan. Adenosine appears to be one cause. It accumulates in the bloodstream during waking hours and is broken down during sleep, which suggests that the body uses it to measure how much sleep has been lost and to set off sleep when the level runs high. Together with other factors, this build-up explains the debt that follows several nights of poor sleep — a debt paid only by sleeping longer than usual. Because the mechanism is built in, no one adjusts to chronic short sleep.
