Production of solar panels requires9 scarce10 and expensive minerals which11 must be excavated12, again requiring13 the use of fossil fuels. Thus, the harder14 we push towards a renewable energy system, the faster we have to use fossil energy for the construction15 process. This is not only expensive, but also an undermining16 factor for our efforts to cut global emissions17. Heinberg remarks that the cost of building18 this new energy infrastructure is seldom19 counted20 in transition proposals, which tend21 to focus just on energy supply requirements.

Yuna: Heinberg’s point is that building a renewable energy system is itself a huge project — one of the biggest in human history.

Dohyun: Not just panels and turbines. New transport systems, new farming methods, new industrial processes — all of it has to be built.

Yuna: And none of that can be built without fossil fuels. Concrete and steel need energy that only fossil fuels can supply right now.

Dohyun: Solar panels too. The minerals have to be mined, and mining burns fossil fuels.

Yuna: So the faster we push toward renewables, the faster we burn fossil energy to build them.

Dohyun: Which is expensive and actually works against cutting emissions in the short run.

Yuna: The strange part is that transition proposals almost never count that construction cost.

Dohyun: Right — they only ask how much energy supply we’ll need afterward.
