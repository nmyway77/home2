다음 글에 대한 <보기>의 설명 중 틀린 것만을 있는 대로 고른 것은?
According to the American journalist Richard Heinberg, putting up the renewable energy system needed to halt global warming makes us participants in one of the largest transformations humanity has ever attempted. Beyond solar panels and wind turbines, a whole new set of transport networks, farming methods and industrial processes has to be created. None of this can be done without fossil fuels. Making concrete and steel, for example, takes quantities of energy that only fossil fuels can currently deliver. The production of solar panels (ⓐ) [call / calls] for rare, costly minerals that have to be dug out of the ground, which once more means burning fossil fuels. So the more forcefully we move toward renewables, the more quickly we burn through fossil energy to build them. Besides being costly, this works against the very goal of lowering global emissions. Heinberg points out that proposals for the energy transition rarely include the cost of constructing the new system; they usually look only at how much energy will have to be supplied.
<보 기>
(A) Concrete, steel and solar-panel minerals are given as examples of why the transition cannot do without fossil fuels.
(B) ‘calls’ is the right choice for (ⓐ), because the subject of the sentence is ‘The production’, not ‘solar panels’.
(C) The sentence ‘the more forcefully we move toward renewables, the more quickly we burn through fossil energy’ means that a faster transition reduces our use of fossil fuels.
(D) In the summary ‘Heinberg argues that the fossil cost of the transition are rarely counted’, the verb ‘are’ agrees with its subject.
①(A), (B)
②(B), (C)
③(A), (D)

Yuna: Heinberg’s point is that building a renewable energy system is itself a huge project — one of the biggest in human history.

Dohyun: Not just panels and turbines. New transport systems, new farming methods, new industrial processes — all of it has to be built.

Yuna: And none of that can be built without fossil fuels. Concrete and steel need energy that only fossil fuels can supply right now.

Dohyun: Solar panels too. The minerals have to be mined, and mining burns fossil fuels.

Yuna: So the faster we push toward renewables, the faster we burn fossil energy to build them.

Dohyun: Which is expensive and actually works against cutting emissions in the short run.

Yuna: The strange part is that transition proposals almost never count that construction cost.

Dohyun: Right — they only ask how much energy supply we’ll need afterward.
