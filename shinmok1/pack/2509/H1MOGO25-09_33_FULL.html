2.7점
윗글의 내용과 일치하지 않는 것은?
Ordinary people struggle with uncertainty partly because how much it matters is not fixed. Consider the Earth-sun distance, 1.49597 × 10⁸ km, taken at one moment in the year. Six significant digits sound impressive: they pin the figure down to roughly one part in a million. Yet if the seventh digit is in doubt, the possible error is greater than the gap between New York and Chicago. So whether the figure counts as precise hangs on the job it has to do. For predicting the minute of tomorrow's sunrise it is more than enough; for steering a satellite into a tight orbit around the sun it is nowhere near enough.

윗글의 내용과 일치하지 않는 것은?
Why is uncertainty so hard for the public to weigh? One reason is that its importance is relative to purpose. The Earth-sun distance, quoted to six significant digits, seems exact — accurate to about one part in a million. But an uncertain next digit means the error could exceed the distance from New York to Chicago. The same figure is therefore both precise and imprecise: fine for someone who only wants to know when the sun will rise, and inadequate for anyone planning to put a satellite in orbit just above the sun.

조건 · 보기
Now suppose the seventh digit is in doubt.
The public has trouble with uncertainty because its importance is never fixed. ( ① ) Take the Earth-sun distance, quoted as 1.49597 × 10⁸ km. ( ② ) With six significant digits, the figure is known to about one part in a million, which sounds very exact. ( ③ ) That single uncertain digit represents an error larger than the distance between New York and Chicago. ( ④ ) So whether the number is precise depends entirely on what it is for. ( ⑤ ) It will do for predicting tomorrow's sunrise; it will not do for sending a satellite into orbit just above the sun.

주어진 글 다음에 이어질 글의 순서로 가장 적절한 것은?
조건 · 보기
Uncertainty is hard for the public to judge because how much it matters is relative.
(A) Which of the two is right? Both — and that is the point: a number is precise or not only in relation to what you intend to do with it.
(B) The Earth-sun distance, given to six significant digits, illustrates this. To someone forecasting sunrise it is more than precise enough.
(C) To someone steering a satellite close to the sun, the same figure is hopelessly rough, since an uncertain seventh digit hides an error larger than the gap between two big cities.
