One of the things that makes uncertainty difficult for members of the public to appreciate1 is that2 the significance3 of uncertainty is relative4. Take, for example, the distance5 between Earth and the sun: 1.49597 × 10⁸ km, as measured6 at one point during the year. This seems relatively precise7; after all, using8 six significant9 digits means I know the distance to an accuracy10 of one part in a million11 or so. However, if the next digit is12 uncertain, that means the uncertainty in knowing13 the precise Earth-sun distance is larger14 than the distance between New York and Chicago! Whether15 or not the quoted number is “precise” therefore depends16 on what I’m intending17 to do with it. If I care only about what minute18 the sun will rise tomorrow, then the number quoted19 here is fine. If I want to send20 a satellite to orbit just above the sun, however, then I would need to know distances more accurately21.

2Take, for example, the distance between Earth and the sun: 1.49597 × 10⁸ km, as measured at one point during the year.
예를 들어 연중 한 시점에 측정된 지구와 태양 사이의 거리 1.49597 × 10⁸ km를 보자.
3This seems relatively precise; after all, using six significant digits means I know the distance to an accuracy of one part in a million or so.
이것은 비교적 정확해 보인다. 결국 유효숫자 여섯 자리를 쓴다는 것은 백만분의 1 정도의 정확도로 그 거리를 안다는 뜻이다.
4However, if the next digit is uncertain, that means the uncertainty in knowing the precise Earth-sun distance is larger than the distance between New York and Chicago!
하지만 다음 자릿수가 불확실하다면, 그것은 정확한 지구-태양 거리를 아는 데 있어서의 불확실성이 뉴욕과 시카고 사이의 거리보다 크다는 뜻이다!
5Whether or not the quoted number is “precise” therefore depends on what I’m intending to do with it.
따라서 인용된 숫자가 ‘정확한지’ 아닌지는 내가 그것으로 무엇을 하려는지에 달려 있다.
6If I care only about what minute the sun will rise tomorrow, then the number quoted here is fine.
내일 해가 몇 분에 뜰지에만 관심이 있다면, 여기 인용된 숫자로 충분하다.
7If I want to send a satellite to orbit just above the sun, however, then I would need to know distances more accurately.
그러나 태양 바로 위 궤도에 위성을 보내고 싶다면, 나는 거리를 더 정확하게 알아야 할 것이다.

The same rule applies in a kitchen. A home cook who measures flour on a kitchen scale is satisfied if the display reads 500 grams, even though the true weight may be a few grams more or less; the cake will not notice. A pharmacist weighing32 the active ingredient for a tablet cannot accept an error of even a few milligrams, since too little will not treat the patient and too much may harm them33. The scale in each case may be equally good; what differs34 is how much error the purpose can tolerate.

33-1
복합 <보기> 판별 (A)~(D)
3.3점
다음 글에 대한 <보기>의 설명 중 틀린 것만을 있는 대로 고른 것은?
Part of what makes uncertainty hard for ordinary people to grasp is that how much an uncertainty matters is never fixed; it changes with the situation. Consider the Earth-sun distance, recorded at one moment of the year as 1.49597 × 10⁸ km. That looks quite exact — six significant figures mean the value is known to roughly one part in a million. Yet if the seventh figure cannot be trusted, the gap between what we know and the true distance is wider than the stretch from New York to Chicago! So whether this figure deserves to be called “precise” comes down to the use (ⓐ) [which / what] I have in mind for it. For someone who merely wants to know the minute of tomorrow’s sunrise, the number is more than good enough. For someone planning to place a satellite in orbit close to the sun, however, far more exact distances would be required.
<보 기>
(A) The Earth-sun distance is used as an example to show that the same number can be precise enough for one purpose and not for another.
(B) ‘which’ is the right choice for (ⓐ), because it refers back to ‘the use’ and serves as the object of ‘have’.
(C) The sentence ‘That looks quite exact’ means the writer is convinced that six significant figures are always sufficient.
(D) In the summary ‘Whether a measurement counts as precise depend on its purpose’, the verb ‘depend’ agrees with its subject.
①(A), (B)
②(B), (C)
③(C), (D)
④(A), (D)
⑤(B), (C), (D)

①A number that sounds precise can hide an error that is enormous by everyday standards.
②An uncertainty in the next digit of such a huge figure amounts to hundreds of kilometers or more.
③The comparison is meant to make an abstract uncertainty feel concrete to the reader.
④The writer believes the measurement is wrong by at least the width of the United States.
⑤The exclamation mark signals that the size of the error is surprising.
