2.7점
밑줄 친 부분이 다음 글에서 의미하는 바로 가장 적절한 것은?
For many children, it is not clear that ‘Right’ is the opposite of ‘Left’. If parents tell their child “Don’t go to the left,” they will often find that the child will continue riding straight ahead and not automatically turn to the right. The same applies to the logical connection between ‘Yes’ and ‘No’ as perceived by parents. When we were children and were told, for example, “No, don’t eat with your hands,” we were confused and didn’t know what our parents expected from us.
①Children also fail to see that hearing ‘No’ points to something they should do instead.
②Parents, too, often mix up ‘Yes’ and ‘No’ when they talk to small children.
③Children learn the word ‘No’ long before they learn the word ‘Yes’.
④Children find ‘Yes’ and ‘No’ easier to tell apart than ‘Left’ and ‘Right’.
⑤Parents say ‘No’ far too often while teaching their children to ride.

3.1점
다음 글의 내용으로 미루어 볼 때, 어린아이에게서 일어날 가능성이 높은 것을 두 개 고르면?
As children, the principle of opposites is foreign: children perceive words and their meanings separately from each other. Told “Don’t go to the left,” a child will continue riding straight ahead and not automatically turn to the right. Told “No, don’t eat with your hands,” a child is confused and doesn’t know what the parents expect.
①Told “Don’t shout,” a small child falls silent and stares at the parent, unsure what to do next.
②Told “Don’t stand up,” a small child sits down at once, exactly as the parent hoped.
③Told “Don’t go up,” a toddler on the stairs simply stays where she is instead of coming down.
④Told “Don’t use the blue cup,” a three-year-old at once reaches for the red one beside it.
⑤Told “Don’t open the door,” a small child closes all the windows in the room as well.

3.0점
다음 글의 밑줄 친 부분 중, 문맥상 낱말의 쓰임이 적절하지 않은 것은?
As children, the principle of opposites is ①foreign. Children perceive words and their meanings ②separately from each other. It is only in ③early development that we understood that individual words directly connect to one another. For many children, for instance, it is not clear that ‘Right’ is the opposite of ‘Left’. A vivid example of this can be seen when children learn to ride a bicycle. If parents tell their child “Don’t go to the left,” they will often find that the child will continue riding straight ahead and not ④automatically turn to the right. The same applies to the logical connection between ‘Yes’ and ‘No’ as perceived by parents. When we were children and were told, for example, “No, don’t eat with your hands,” we were ⑤confused and didn’t know what our parents expected from us. Our confusion was about whether we should continue eating or not and if so, how? Only later did we recognize the connection and understand that we should continue eating, but not with our hands but with a fork or a spoon.

Children perceive words and their meanings separately from each other, and it is only in later development that we understand that individual words directly connect to one another. For many children it is not clear that ‘Right’ is the opposite of ‘Left’. If parents tell their child on a bicycle “Don’t go to the left,” the child will often continue riding straight ahead. 주제
2.7점
다음 글의 주제로 가장 적절한 것은?
Adults take it for granted that ‘not left’ means ‘right’ and that ‘No’ to one thing means ‘Yes’ to another. Children do not. They perceive each word and its meaning separately, and only in later development do they come to see how words connect to one another. That is why a child on a bicycle who hears “Don’t go to the left” keeps riding straight ahead, and why a child who hears “No, don’t eat with your hands” has no idea whether to stop eating or to carry on in some other way.
①the best age at which children should learn to ride a bicycle
②children’s late grasp of how words relate to each other as opposites
③the lasting harm that negative commands do to young children
④differences in the way mothers and fathers give instructions
⑤the part that table manners play in learning a first language
