Kivetz, Urminsky, and Zheng partnered1 with a café to test the motivating2 effect of illusory3 progress in an experiment. Customers received a reward card that4 offered one free coffee after they’d bought ten5. While half of the customers received a card with ten open slots, the other half got a card with twelve open slots6. Yet the twelve-slot card had two preexisting7 “bonus” stamps, so, strictly speaking, these were8 identical9 reward programs. Every customer who got a card needed to make10 ten coffee purchases (and collect ten stamps) to get their free coffee. But the appeal of the free stamps was high11. People who thought they’d gotten a head start came back to the café more often, filling12 in their reward card more quickly13 than the others. When the card came with two out of twelve slots already filled, it felt to customers like they were already 16 percent14 finished with the goal before they’d even started15. Believing16 they were closer to the reward, they were more motivated to reach17 the finish line. According to the study above, customers were more driven18 to complete their goals19 when they perceived20 the reward as nearer21.

Kivetz, Urminsky, and Zheng teamed up with a café to run an experiment on how the feeling of progress—even when it is only an illusion—motivates people. Each customer was given a loyalty card promising a free coffee once ten coffees had been bought. Half of the customers got a card with ten empty slots; the other half got one with twelve. However, the twelve-slot card already carried two ‘bonus’ stamps, which meant that, in strict terms, the two programs were exactly the same. Whoever held a card had to buy ten coffees (and collect ten stamps) before the free one. Still, the free stamps proved very attractive. Those who felt they had been given a head start returned to the café more often and completed their cards faster than the rest. With two of twelve slots filled from the outset, customers felt they were already 16 percent of the way to the goal before making a single purchase. Since they believed the reward was nearer, they tried harder to get there. The study shows that people work harder toward a goal when the reward seems closer to them.

( ⑤ ) Believing they were closer to the reward, they were more motivated to reach the finish line. OP0삽입문 = 원문 S6(But the appeal of the free stamps was high.) · 단서: But(앞 ‘사실은 같은 프로그램’과 역접) → 뒤 People who thought … (매력의 결과)
1. 📖 문제 해석주어진 문장(그러나 공짜 도장의 매력은 컸다)이 들어갈 곳을 찾는다.
2. 📖 지문/근거 해석But은 앞의 ‘엄밀히 같은 프로그램 · 모두 열 번 사야 한다’(실질적 차이 없음)와 역접을 이루고, 뒤에는 그 매력의 결과(먼저 출발했다고 생각한 사람들이 더 자주 돌아옴)가 와야 한다 → ③.
3. 📖 조건/선지 해석③ 앞: 두 카드가 실제로는 같다는 설명(두 문장). ③ 뒤: 그럼에도 행동이 달라진 결과.
