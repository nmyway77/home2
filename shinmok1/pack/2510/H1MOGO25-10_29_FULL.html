3.2점
[A]와 [B]를 읽고 이해한 것으로 가장 적절한 것은?
[A] Nutritional science is still young and there is so much we do not know. If we have eaten certain foods in certain ways for millennia, we should assume until it is proven otherwise that there is probably a good reason why. [B] In farming, too, haste can be a form of arrogance. In many regions farmers kept growing the local grain varieties their ancestors had selected over countless seasons, even when newer seeds promised faster growth. The old varieties had come through the droughts, pests, and poor soils of that particular place, and their persistence was itself a kind of knowledge. Where villages replaced them all at once with a single imported variety, one bad year could wipe out an entire harvest. The lesson is not that new seeds are worthless, but that a variety which has fed a region for generations deserves the benefit of the doubt until it is clearly shown to be inferior.
①[A] blames nutritional scientists for the decline of traditional societies, while [B] blames farmers for abandoning their seeds.
②Both texts hold that whatever is new is bound to be worse than whatever is old.
③Both texts argue that something which has lasted for a very long time should be trusted until it is proven otherwise — [A] about foods, [B] about crop varieties.
④[A] treats a rapid change of diet as an experiment, whereas [B] sees replacing old seeds gradually as no safer than replacing them all at once.
⑤[B] regards the survival of old varieties as mere luck, while [A] regards a food’s long use as evidence of a reason behind it.
