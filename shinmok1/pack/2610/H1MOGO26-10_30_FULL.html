Subsidies, taxes, and regulations can all lead to power plants that1 pollute less2 by switching3 away from coal. In all cases, profits4 can be higher from making the transition5, even taking6 the related costs into account. The power plants prefer7 subsidies over regulations or taxes. The reason is obvious8. They prefer to receive money from the government rather than pay9 the costs themselves. But the “expansion” of their opportunity set through subsidies has to be matched10 by the contraction11 of the opportunity set of others. The electricity companies’ “freedom” is increased12 at the expense13 of the ordinary individual, who14 must suffer from increased15 taxation to pay for the subsidy. Moreover, from the perspective of pure efficiency, the subsidy distorts16 the economy because the electricity company is not facing the full costs of what it does, even if it is17 successful in limiting18 emissions. Indeed, electricity prices may end up lower than they should be and electricity consumption higher, which will worsen19 global warming, even though the emissions per unit of electricity have20 been reduced21.

본문 문장별 해석영어 한 문장 · 우리말 한 문장
1Subsidies, taxes, and regulations can all lead to power plants that pollute less by switching away from coal.
보조금, 세금, 그리고 규제는 모두 석탄 사용에서 벗어나는 전환을 통해 오염을 덜 일으키는 발전소로 이어질 수 있다.
2In all cases, profits can be higher from making the transition, even taking the related costs into account.
모든 경우에, 관련 비용까지 고려하더라도, 그 전환을 하는 것에서 얻는 이익은 더 높을 수 있다.
3The power plants prefer subsidies over regulations or taxes.
발전소들은 규제나 세금보다 보조금을 선호한다.
4The reason is obvious.
그 이유는 명백하다.
5They prefer to receive money from the government rather than pay the costs themselves.
그들은 그 비용을 스스로 지불하기보다는 정부로부터 돈을 받는 것을 선호한다.
6But the “expansion” of their opportunity set through subsidies has to be matched by the contraction of the opportunity set of others.
그러나 보조금을 통한 그들의 기회 집합의 ‘확대’는 다른 사람들의 기회 집합의 축소로 맞추어져야만 한다.
7The electricity companies’ “freedom” is increased at the expense of the ordinary individual, who must suffer from increased taxation to pay for the subsidy.
전력 회사들의 ‘자유’는 일반 개인을 희생시켜 늘어나는데, 그 개인은 그 보조금을 지급하기 위해 늘어난 세금으로 고통을 겪어야 한다.
8Moreover, from the perspective of pure efficiency, the subsidy distorts the economy because the electricity company is not facing the full costs of what it does, even if it is successful in limiting emissions.
더욱이 순수한 효율성의 관점에서 보면, 보조금은 경제를 왜곡하는데, 전력 회사가 설령 배출량을 제한하는 데 성공하더라도 자신이 하는 일의 전체 비용을 직면하고 있지 않기 때문이다.
9Indeed, electricity prices may end up lower than they should be and electricity consumption higher, which will worsen global warming, even though the emissions per unit of electricity have been reduced.

Whether a government uses subsidies, taxes, or rules, each of these tools can push power plants to cut their pollution by moving away from coal. In every case, making the change can leave a plant more profitable even after the costs involved are counted. Still, the plants would much rather have subsidies than rules or taxes, and it is easy to see why: getting money from the government beats paying the bill on their own. Yet whenever subsidies widen the range of choices open to the plants, the range open to someone else must shrink by the same amount. The electricity firms gain their ‘freedom’ at the cost of the ordinary citizen, (ⓐ) [whose / which] taxes go up to fund the subsidy. There is also a problem of pure efficiency: a subsidized company never confronts the true cost of its own actions, so the subsidy bends the economy out of shape even when it does manage to cut emissions. In the end, electricity may become cheaper than it ought to be and people may use more of it, so that global warming gets worse although each unit of electricity now produces fewer emissions.

[A]와 [B]를 읽고 이해한 것으로 가장 적절하지 않은 것은?
[A] Subsidies, taxes, and regulations can all lead to power plants that pollute less by switching away from coal. The power plants prefer subsidies over regulations or taxes. But the “expansion” of their opportunity set through subsidies has to be matched by the contraction of the opportunity set of others. The company naturally welcomes the gift, since it does not pay for the passes itself. Yet the money comes from every other resident, whose taxes rise to cover the cost. And because the company never sees the full price of its choice, it may hire more drivers than the city’s roads can handle.
①Both [A] and [B] describe a benefit given to one party that is paid for by many other people.
②In [A] the cost falls on taxpayers, and in [B] it falls on the other residents of the city.
③Both texts suggest that the party receiving the benefit does not face the full cost of its own choices.
④[A] points to a possible climate outcome, whereas [B] points to a possible traffic outcome.
⑤[A] blames the government for choosing subsidies, and [B] blames the workers for giving up their cars.

Whether a government uses subsidies, taxes, or rules, each of these tools can push power plants to cut their pollution by moving away from coal. In every case, making the change can leave a plant more profitable even after the costs involved are counted. Still, the plants would much rather have subsidies than rules or taxes, and it is easy to see why: getting money from the government beats paying the bill on their own. Yet whenever subsidies widen the range of choices open to the plants, the range open to someone else must shrink by the same amount. The electricity firms gain their ‘freedom’ at the cost of the ordinary citizen, whose taxes go up to fund the subsidy. There is also a problem of pure efficiency: a subsidized company never confronts the true cost of its own actions, so the subsidy bends the economy out of shape even when it does manage to cut emissions. In the end, electricity may become cheaper than it ought to be and people may use more of it, so that global warming gets worse although each unit of electricity now produces fewer emissions.

주어진 글 다음에 이어질 글의 순서로 가장 적절한 것은?
Subsidies, taxes, and regulations can all lead to power plants that pollute less by switching away from coal. In all cases, profits can be higher from making the transition, even taking the related costs into account.

(A) But the “expansion” of their opportunity set through subsidies has to be matched by the contraction of the opportunity set of others. The electricity companies’ “freedom” is increased at the expense of the ordinary individual, who must suffer from increased taxation to pay for the subsidy.

(B) The power plants prefer subsidies over regulations or taxes. The reason is obvious. They prefer to receive money from the government rather than pay the costs themselves.

(C) Moreover, from the perspective of pure efficiency, the subsidy distorts the economy because the electricity company is not facing the full costs of what it does, even if it is successful in limiting emissions. Indeed, electricity prices may end up lower than they should be and electricity consumption higher, which will worsen global warming, even though the emissions per unit of electricity have been reduced.

①(A) - (C) - (B)
②(B) - (A) - (C)
③(B) - (C) - (A)
④(C) - (A) - (B)
⑤(C) - (B) - (A)

정답 ②
OP0원문 3분할 · 단서: 주어진 글(세 도구 모두 효과·이익) → (B) 그중 보조금 선호 + 이유 → (A) But 보조금의 확대 = 남의 축소 → (C) Moreover 효율성 + Indeed 결과
1. 📖 문제 해석주어진 글(세 도구 모두 효과가 있고 이익도 남는다) 뒤에 이어질 순서를 지시어·연결어 단서로 정한다.
2. 📖 지문/근거 해석(B)는 세 도구 중 발전소가 ‘보조금’을 고른다는 전환 — 주어진 글의 세 도구를 받는다. (A)의 their opportunity set은 (B)의 the power plants를 받고, But으로 보조금의 대가(남의 축소·세금)를 제시. (C)의 Moreover는 (A)의 비판(공정성)에 효율성 비판을 ‘더하고’, Indeed로 결과를 맺는다.
