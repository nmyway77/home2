The very fact that1 all of our experiences “take place” in a physical space makes2 the space inseparable3 from what defines it, from the event that took place in it. The tree and the ground beneath are inseparable from what4 took place there. This intertwining5 of space, place, and experience challenges6 the idea of place as passive7 backdrop. If place and space are central8 to the very heart of experience, we must accept the possibility that the spatial framework is the medium9 of all experiences — actions, thoughts, memories, and feelings of every place — not merely10 a background or context exterior11 to experience (as commonly supposed12). Belgian literary critic Georges Poulet made the point elegantly13, writing14 of the space created15 in Marcel Proust’s great novel In Search of Lost Time: “Beings surround themselves16 with the places where17 they find themselves, the way one wraps oneself up in a garment18 that is at one and the same time a disguise19 and a characterization. Without places, beings would20 be only abstractions21.”

33-1
복합 <보기> 판별 (A)~(D)
3.3점
다음 글에 대한 <보기>의 설명 중 틀린 것만을 있는 대로 고른 것은?
Because every one of our experiences happens somewhere physical, that place cannot be separated from what gives it meaning — the event that occurred in it. A tree and the earth under it cannot be pulled apart from what happened on that spot. When space, place, and experience are woven together like this, we can no longer treat a place as a mere passive setting. If place sits at the core of experience, then we have to allow that the spatial frame may be the very medium through which all experience — our actions, thoughts, memories, and feelings in each place — is lived, rather than a background that stands outside experience, as people usually assume. The Belgian critic Georges Poulet put this gracefully when he described the space in Marcel Proust’s novel In Search of Lost Time: people wrap themselves in the places (ⓐ) [which / where] they occupy, just as a person wraps up in a coat that both hides and reveals who they are, and without places they would be nothing but abstractions.
<보 기>
(A) The tree and the earth are used as an example of a place that cannot be separated from what happened there.
(B) ‘which’ is the right choice for (ⓐ), because ‘occupy’ needs an object and the clause lacks one.
(C) The phrase ‘as people usually assume’ refers to the writer’s own view that place is the medium of experience.
(D) In the summary ‘Place and the events that happen in it are impossible to separate’, the verb ‘are’ agrees with its subject.
①(B)
②(C)
③(A), (C)
④(B), (C)
⑤(C), (D)

[A]와 [B]를 읽고 이해한 것으로 가장 적절하지 않은 것은?
[A] The very fact that all of our experiences “take place” in a physical space makes the space inseparable from what defines it, from the event that took place in it. The tree and the ground beneath are inseparable from what took place there. When space, place, and experience are woven together like this, we can no longer treat a place as a mere passive setting. It becomes part of the experience itself, which is why a tune can seem to hold a whole afternoon.
①Both [A] and [B] reject the view that their subject is merely something that surrounds an experience from outside.
②In [A] the subject is physical place, and in [B] it is music that accompanied a moment.
③Both texts suggest that the subject and the experience become so tied together that one carries the other.
④[B] gives a specific, everyday example, whereas [A] supports its claim with a literary critic’s words.
⑤[A] says that places disguise who we really are, and [B] says that music makes us forget what really happened.

문장 삽입
2.5점
글의 흐름으로 보아, 주어진 문장이 들어가기에 가장 적절한 곳은?
The tree and the ground beneath are inseparable from what took place there.
( ① ) The very fact that all of our experiences “take place” in a physical space makes the space inseparable from what defines it, from the event that took place in it. ( ② ) This intertwining of space, place, and experience challenges the idea of place as passive backdrop. ( ③ ) If place and space are central to the very heart of experience, we must accept the possibility that the spatial framework is the medium of all experiences — actions, thoughts, memories, and feelings of every place — not merely a background or context exterior to experience (as commonly supposed). ( ④ ) Belgian literary critic Georges Poulet made the point elegantly, writing of the space created in Marcel Proust’s great novel In Search of Lost Time: “Beings surround themselves with the places where they find themselves, the way one wraps oneself up in a garment that is at one and the same time a disguise and a characterization. ( ⑤ ) Without places, beings would be only abstractions.”

33-8 정답 & 4단계 해설
정답 ②
OP0삽입 문장 = S2(The tree and the ground beneath …) · 단서: 앞 문장의 inseparable from … the event that took place in it을 구체적 예로 반복 + 뒤 This intertwining이 ‘이것’으로 받음 · 함정 ③
1. 📖 문제 해석주어진 문장이 들어갈 위치를 지시어·반복 표현 단서로 정한다.
2. 📖 지문/근거 해석주어진 문장은 ‘장소는 그곳에서 일어난 일과 분리될 수 없다’를 나무와 땅이라는 구체적 예로 되풀이한다(inseparable from what took place there ↔ inseparable from … the event that took place in it). 따라서 일반 진술(S1) 바로 뒤. 그다음 문장의 This intertwining은 ‘공간·장소·경험의 뒤얽힘’이라는 두 문장을 함께 받는다 → ②.
3. 📖 조건/선지 해석① 일반 진술보다 예가 먼저 와서 inseparable의 뜻이 불명확 ② 일반 진술 → 구체적 예 → This로 받음 ○ ③ This intertwining 뒤에 오면 This가 받을 ‘예’가 아직 없고, If절 주장 사이에 끼어 흐름이 끊김 ④·⑤ Poulet 인용부 안으로 들어가 글의 예시가 인용문처럼 됨
4. ✦ 정답 도출 + 오답 함정③ [한 끗] ‘뒤얽힘’ 문장 바로 뒤도 예시 자리처럼 보이지만, 그러면 This intertwining이 가리킬 구체적 장면이 앞에 없어진다 — This는 앞 두 문장을 받는다. ① 첫 문장이 inseparable을 먼저 정의해야 ‘그곳에서 일어난 일’이 무엇인지 알 수 있다.
