All sound begins with some kind of motion1, something that2 disturbs3 molecules in air, water, steel, wood, or other medium. Isaac Newton noted that4 light waves are colorless5; the perception6 of color only occurs in the brain of a living organism7. The same is true of sound. Thus perception of sound begins at our eardrums8 — before it hits our eardrums, it is nothing more9 than the disturbance10 of molecules. We can measure that disturbance with gauges and instruments, but until11 it hits a brain, it is not sound. You may have heard12 the old philosophical13 riddle, “If a tree falls14 in the forest and no one is there to hear15 it, does it make it sound?” No, because sound is a psychological16 concept, a product17 of brains. That tree will18 disturb molecules and make a mess19, but if no creature20 hears it, no bird, fox, or human, it has not made21 sound.

36-1
복합 <보기> 판별 (A)~(D)
3.3점
다음 글에 대한 <보기>의 설명 중 틀린 것만을 있는 대로 고른 것은?
Every sound starts as movement of some kind — something (ⓐ) [that / what] shakes the molecules of air, water, steel, wood, or whatever substance carries it. Isaac Newton pointed out that light waves themselves have no color; color is sensed only inside the brain of a living creature. Sound works in exactly the same way. Our experience of sound therefore starts at the eardrum, and before anything reaches the eardrum it is merely molecules being pushed around. Instruments can record that disturbance, yet it does not become sound until it arrives at a brain. You have probably come across the old puzzle: if a tree falls in a forest with nobody around to hear it, is there a sound? The answer is no, since sound is a mental event, something brains produce. The falling tree will shake molecules and leave a mess, but unless some living thing — a bird, a fox, a person — hears it, it has produced no sound at all.
<보 기>
(A) Newton’s remark about color is used to prepare the reader for the same claim about sound.
(B) ‘that’ is the right choice for (ⓐ), because ‘something’ is its antecedent and the clause has no subject of its own.
(C) ‘The answer is no, since sound is a mental event, something brains produce.’ means that the falling tree does make a sound, but a very quiet one.
(D) In the summary ‘Neither color nor sound exists outside a living brain’, the verb ‘exists’ agrees with its subject.
①(A)
②(C)
③(B), (C)
④(A), (D)
⑤(C), (D)

( ⑤ ) That tree will disturb molecules and make a mess, but if no creature hears it, no bird, fox, or human, it has not made sound.

정답 ②
OP0삽입 문장 = S3(The same is true of sound.) · 단서: 앞 = 뉴턴의 색 이야기, 뒤 = Thus perception of sound … (소리로 적용) · 함정 ① (첫 문장 뒤)
1. 📖 문제 해석주어진 문장이 들어갈 위치를 지시어·연결어 단서로 정한다.
2. 📖 지문/근거 해석The same(같은 것)이 받을 내용은 ‘빛에는 색이 없고 색은 뇌에서만 지각된다’는 뉴턴의 말이어야 한다. 그리고 바로 뒤 Thus perception of sound begins at our eardrums는 ‘그러므로 소리의 지각도 …’라며 그 원리를 소리에 적용한다 → 뉴턴 문장과 Thus 문장 사이 ②.
3. 📖 조건/선지 해석① 첫 문장은 소리 이야기이므로 ‘소리도 마찬가지’가 받을 다른 대상(색)이 아직 없음 ② 색(뉴턴) → 소리도 같다 → Thus 소리의 지각 ○ ③ Thus 문장이 먼저 나오면 ‘그러므로’의 근거가 없음 ④·⑤ 이미 소리 이야기가 한참 진행된 뒤라 The same의 비교 대상이 멀어짐
4. ✦ 정답 도출 + 오답 함정① [한 끗] 첫 문장 뒤도 자연스러워 보이지만, The same은 ‘소리가 아닌 다른 것’에 대한 진술 뒤에 와야 한다 — 첫 문장은 이미 소리 이야기. ③ Thus가 결론 접속어이므로 그 앞에 근거(색과 같다)가 있어야 한다.
