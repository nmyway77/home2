Patterns such as infinite scroll, autoplaying videos, or requiring an account just to make a purchase all interfere1 with the user’s control2 over their own experience. From a sustainability3 perspective, many of these patterns encourage4 overconsumption5 of data and attention. Infinite scroll keeps loading6 new content whether the user wants it or not, increasing data usage and device processing. Autoplay forces media to load and play even for people who7 may never watch it. These choices consume more energy on both the user’s device and the servers delivering8 the content. Take the decision by a popular music streaming platform to replace static album artwork with short video loops by default9. This change significantly10 increases data usage and battery consumption, while the option to disable it is buried deep in the settings. As a result, most users consume more resources11 unless they actively search for a way to opt out. It12 is important to acknowledge13 that web design always nudges behavior in some way. Showing consequences and highlighting options are14 all forms of influence. The ethical line is not whether nudging exists, but whether it is transparent15, reversible16, and aligned with the user’s interests. In many cases, the most responsible approach is to make the lower-impact option the default, while keeping alternatives clearly available17. Defaults are powerful, and when chosen18 carefully, they can reduce19 environmental impact without removing20 user agency21.

41-1
복합 <보기> 판별 (A)~(D)
3.3점
다음 글에 대한 <보기>의 설명 중 틀린 것만을 있는 대로 고른 것은?
Design patterns like endless scrolling, videos that start by themselves, or demanding an account before a single purchase all take control of the experience away from the user. Seen through the lens of sustainability, many of these patterns push people to use up more data and more attention than they need. Endless scrolling goes on pulling in fresh content whether or not the user asked for it, which drives up data traffic and the work the device has to do. Autoplay makes media download and run even for people who will never look at it. Every such choice burns extra energy both on the user’s device and on the servers (ⓐ) [that / what] send the content. Consider what happened when a well-known music streaming service decided to show short looping videos instead of still album covers as the standard setting. The switch pushed up data use and battery drain considerably, and the control for turning it off was tucked far down in the settings. Consequently, most users end up spending more resources unless they go looking for a way out. We need to admit that web design always steers behavior in one direction or another. Displaying consequences and emphasizing certain choices are themselves kinds of influence. What decides whether a nudge is ethical is not its existence but whether it is open, undoable, and in line with what the user wants. Often the most responsible path is to set the gentler option as the default and keep the other options plainly within reach. Defaults carry great weight, and chosen with care they can cut environmental harm without taking choice away from the user.

주어진 글 다음에 이어질 글의 순서로 가장 적절한 것은?
Patterns such as infinite scroll, autoplaying videos, or requiring an account just to make a purchase all interfere with the user’s control over their own experience. From a sustainability perspective, many of these patterns encourage overconsumption of data and attention.

(A) Take the decision by a popular music streaming platform to replace static album artwork with short video loops by default. This change significantly increases data usage and battery consumption, while the option to disable it is buried deep in the settings. As a result, most users consume more resources unless they actively search for a way to opt out.

(B) Infinite scroll keeps loading new content whether the user wants it or not, increasing data usage and device processing. Autoplay forces media to load and play even for people who may never watch it. These choices consume more energy on both the user’s device and the servers delivering the content.

(C) It is important to acknowledge that web design always nudges behavior in some way. Showing consequences and highlighting options are all forms of influence. The ethical line is not whether nudging exists, but whether it is transparent, reversible, and aligned with the user’s interests. In many cases, the most responsible approach is to make the lower-impact option the default, while keeping alternatives clearly available. Defaults are powerful, and when chosen carefully, they can reduce environmental impact without removing user agency.

②(B) - (A) - (C)
③(B) - (C) - (A)
④(C) - (A) - (B)
⑤(C) - (B) - (A)
