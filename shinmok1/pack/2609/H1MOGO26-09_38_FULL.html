To harness1 the full potential2 of units of measurement3, we need some degree of consistency4. Subjective5 measures contain useful knowledge, but this can be difficult6 to share, based7 as it is on an individual’s experience. The historian of science Theodore M. Porter describes8 measurement as a ‘technology of distance9’: a tool that10 uses shared11 rules to bridge differences of culture and geography, allowing12 for the exchange of information13. Following this logic, if measurement is another sort of language, then just as with words, individual units need reliable14 definitions15 in order to communicate. In the earliest societies, these definitions might need to be shared16 no more widely than a single settlement17, meaning that the definition can be no more complex18 than knowing that a unit was equal to a certain body part. As these communities grew19 in size, though, the inadequacies20 of this system would become evident21.

과학사학자 Theodore M. Porter는 측정을 ‘거리의 기술’, 즉 공유된 규칙을 사용해 문화와 지리의 차이를 이어 정보 교환을 가능하게 하는 도구라고 설명한다.
