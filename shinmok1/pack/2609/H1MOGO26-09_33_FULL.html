The ancient Greeks felt that the visual artist’s goal was1 to copy2 visual experience. This approach appears in the realism3 of ancient Greek sculpture and pottery. We must sadly note that, due to4 the action of time and weather, no paintings from ancient Greek artists exist5 today. We can only surmise6 their quality based7 on tales such as that8 of Zeuxis and Parhassios, the obvious9 skill in ancient Greek sculpture, and in drawings that10 survive on ancient Greek pottery. This definition11 of art as copying12 reality has a problem, though. Jackson Pollock, a leader13 in the New York School of the 1950’s, intentionally14 did not copy existing15 objects in his art. While painting16 these works, Pollock and his fellow artists would consciously17 avoid making18 marks or passages that resembled recognizable objects. They succeeded at making artwork that did not copy anything, thus demonstrating19 that the ancient Greek view of art as mimesis20—simple copying—does not sufficiently21 define art.

In the view of the ancient Greeks, a painter or sculptor had one task: to reproduce what the eye sees. That belief is visible in the lifelike style of Greek statues and painted pottery. Unfortunately, time and weather have destroyed every painting the Greeks produced, so none remains for us to study. All we can do is estimate how good those paintings were from stories like the one about Zeuxis and Parhassios, from the evident mastery of Greek sculpture, and from the drawings still found on Greek pots. Yet defining art as the copying of reality runs into trouble. In the 1950s Jackson Pollock, a leading figure of the New York School, deliberately refused to reproduce any existing object in his work. As they painted, Pollock and the artists around him took care not to make any mark or passage that looked like something recognizable. They managed to create works that copied nothing at all, and in doing so they showed that the Greek idea of art as mimesis—plain copying—is not enough to define what art is.

4. ✦ 정답 도출 + 오답 함정① [지시어 함정] This definition이 첫 문장을 받으므로 ①도 가능해 보이지만, 그러면 ‘문제가 있다’ 뒤에 그 정의를 옹호하는 realism 설명이 이어져 though의 방향과 어긋난다.
