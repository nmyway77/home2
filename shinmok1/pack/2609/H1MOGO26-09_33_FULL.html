This definition of art as copying reality has a problem, though. Jackson Pollock, a leader in the New York School of the 1950’s, intentionally did not copy existing objects in his art. They succeeded at making artwork that did not copy anything, thus demonstrating that the ancient Greek view of art as mimesis—simple copying—does not sufficiently define art.
