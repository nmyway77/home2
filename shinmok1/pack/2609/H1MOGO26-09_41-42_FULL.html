The role of ‘slang’ in popular culture has1 fundamentally2 changed because of the internet. Since the emergence3 of Standard English, the rule makers of language have been4 in charge, and their prescriptive5 norms about a ‘correct’ version of the English language have dominated our channels of communication6. If you wanted your voice to be heard7 by the public, you had to work within their limits, conforming8 to the strict9 expectations of either print or broadcast media. Social media completely broke that barrier10. The playing field is now level11, and public access is democratized12. Anybody and everybody can become a public figure on the new communicative platforms, meaning13 that nobody’s left to enforce14 the old rules. TV presenters and journalists might not have been able to use slang, but content creators can. We regularly use words like ‘function’ and ‘side-eye,’ and our influence both legitimizes15 these terms and gives16 them a large platform to spread quicker than they could have before. Content creators also come from a greater variety17 of backgrounds than people working in traditional media. This means that slang can more naturally18 go beyond the economic and social barriers that19 once limited its growth. The loose social networks that previously connected us geographically are20 simply stronger online, and memes are now transmitted at rapid pace21.

1The role of ‘slang’ in popular culture has fundamentally changed because of the internet.
‘속어’가 대중문화에서 맡는 역할은 인터넷 때문에 근본적으로 바뀌었다.
2Since the emergence of Standard English, the rule makers of language have been in charge, and their prescriptive norms about a ‘correct’ version of the English language have dominated our channels of communication.
표준 영어가 등장한 이래로 언어의 규칙 제정자들이 주도권을 쥐어 왔고, 영어의 ‘올바른’ 형태에 관한 그들의 규범적 기준은 우리의 소통 경로를 지배해 왔다.
3If you wanted your voice to be heard by the public, you had to work within their limits, conforming to the strict expectations of either print or broadcast media.
대중에게 목소리가 들리기를 원한다면, 인쇄나 방송 매체의 엄격한 기대에 맞추며 그들의 한계 안에서 일해야 했다.
4Social media completely broke that barrier.
소셜 미디어는 그 장벽을 완전히 무너뜨렸다.
5The playing field is now level, and public access is democratized.
이제 경기장은 평평해졌고 대중에 대한 접근은 민주화되었다.
6Anybody and everybody can become a public figure on the new communicative platforms, meaning that nobody’s left to enforce the old rules.
누구든 새로운 소통 플랫폼에서 공인이 될 수 있으며, 이는 옛 규칙을 강제할 사람이 아무도 남지 않았음을 뜻한다.
7TV presenters and journalists might not have been able to use slang, but content creators can.
TV 진행자와 기자들은 속어를 쓰지 못했을지 모르지만, 콘텐츠 제작자는 쓸 수 있다.
8We regularly use words like ‘function’ and ‘side-eye,’ and our influence both legitimizes these terms and gives them a large platform to spread quicker than they could have before.
우리는 ‘function’이나 ‘side-eye’ 같은 말을 일상적으로 쓰고, 우리의 영향력은 이 용어들을 정당화하는 동시에 예전보다 더 빨리 퍼질 수 있는 큰 무대를 마련해 준다.
9Content creators also come from a greater variety of backgrounds than people working in traditional media.
콘텐츠 제작자들은 또한 전통 매체에서 일하는 사람들보다 더 다양한 배경 출신이다.
10This means that slang can more naturally go beyond the economic and social barriers that once limited its growth.
이는 속어가 한때 그 성장을 제한했던 경제적·사회적 장벽을 더 자연스럽게 넘을 수 있음을 뜻한다.
11The loose social networks that previously connected us geographically are simply stronger online, and memes are now transmitted at rapid pace.

The internet has completely transformed the part that slang plays in popular culture. Ever since Standard English appeared, those who set the rules of language have held power, and their norms about what counts as ‘correct’ English have ruled the ways we communicate. To reach the public with your own voice, you had to stay inside the limits they set, matching the strict demands of print or broadcast media. Then social media knocked that wall down entirely. Today the field is even, and access to the public has been opened to all. Anyone at all can become a public figure on the new platforms, which means that no one remains to police the old rules. Television hosts and reporters may have been barred from using slang, but people who create online content are not. Online creators also come from a far wider range of backgrounds than those employed by traditional media. The role of ‘slang’ in popular culture has fundamentally changed because of the internet. ( ① ) Since the emergence of Standard English, the rule makers of language have been in charge, and their prescriptive norms about a ‘correct’ version of the English language have dominated our channels of communication. ( ② ) If you wanted your voice to be heard by the public, you had to work within their limits, conforming to the strict expectations of either print or broadcast media. ( ③ ) The playing field is now level, and public access is democratized. ( ④ ) Anybody and everybody can become a public figure on the new communicative platforms, meaning that nobody’s left to enforce the old rules. ( ⑤ ) TV presenters and journalists might not have been able to use slang, but content creators can.
