Keep your paragraphs short1. Writing is visual2—it catches the eye before it has a chance to catch3 the brain. Short paragraphs put air around what4 you write and make it look inviting, whereas a long chunk of type can discourage5 a reader from even starting6 to read. Newspaper paragraphs should be only two or three sentences long; newspaper type is set7 in a narrow8 width, and the inches quickly add9 up. You may think such frequent paragraphing10 will damage11 the development of your point. Obviously The New Yorker is obsessed12 by this fear—a reader can go for miles without relief13. Don’t worry; the gains14 far outweigh the hazards15. But don’t go too far. A succession of tiny paragraphs is as annoying16 as a paragraph that’s17 too long. I’m thinking of all those tiny paragraphs—verbless wonders18—written19 by modern journalists trying to make their articles quick ’n’ easy. Actually they make the reader’s job harder20 by chopping21 up a natural train of thought.

기준 적용 사례 둘 고르기 (AND)
3.3점
다음 글의 필자가 제시한 기준에 맞게 글을 쓴 사람만을 있는 대로 고른 것은?
Make your paragraphs brief. Writing works through the eye first—the page strikes the reader before the mind has time to react. Brief paragraphs surround your words with space and give the page a welcoming look, while one solid block of print can put a reader off before the first line. In a newspaper, paragraphs should run no more than two or three sentences, because the columns are narrow and the lines pile up fast. Perhaps you worry that breaking the text so often will weaken the way your argument unfolds. The New Yorker clearly suffers from that worry—its readers can travel page after page with no breathing room. Set the worry aside; what you gain far exceeds what you risk. Still, there is a limit. A string of tiny paragraphs irritates as much as one that never ends. I have in mind those one-line, verbless fragments that today’s journalists produce in the hope of making their pieces quick and easy. In fact they make reading more difficult, because they slice a natural line of thought into pieces.

④ Hyun keeps his paragraphs short, but when two sentences complete one thought he keeps them together, even if the paragraph runs a little longer.
⑤ Taeho makes every paragraph exactly one line long and breaks sentences in the middle to make them fit.
