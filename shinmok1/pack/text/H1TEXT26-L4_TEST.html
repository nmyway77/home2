The idea that mealworms might help with plastic waste first drew wide attention in 2015, when a team of researchers from China and the United States, including Wei-Min Wu of Stanford University, reported its findings in the journal Environmental Science and Technology. In the laboratory, 100 mealworms ate between 34 and 39 milligrams of Styrofoam every day. The worms turned about half of it into carbon dioxide, and within 24 hours they excreted most of the rest as tiny biodegraded fragments. Surprisingly, mealworms that had eaten nothing but Styrofoam for a month were as healthy as those raised on a normal diet. To find out what made this possible, the scientists gave some worms an antibiotic that suppressed their gut bacteria; these worms lost the ability to break down the plastic. Before this study, the same team had shown that waxworms carry gut microorganisms able to biodegrade polyethylene, another common plastic.

단락 순서
3.0점
주어진 글 (A) 다음에 이어질 글의 순서로 가장 적절한 것은?
(A) Our environment is in serious trouble. As the planet warms, sea levels are climbing, and natural disasters such as wildfires and floods are occurring more frequently. Plastic, in particular, contaminates both soil and water. Humans are clearly a leading cause of this destruction, and reversing it appears daunting. Nature, however, may already have supplied us with answers, and progress is under way in three fields.

(B) Plastic is not the only thing nature can help us clean up. Soil contaminated by mining and other industries can be dug up and hauled to a landfill, but doing so is costly and merely relocates the problem. A greener and cheaper remedy is to plant willow trees. Thanks to their extensive root systems, willows draw a wide range of harmful substances out of the ground, and they thrive even in highly acidic soil. Since some species absorb pollutants better than others, further research is required to identify the most effective ones.

(C) A third answer comes from an unlikely place — the coffee cup. Used coffee grounds are usually sent to landfills, where they release a harmful greenhouse gas. Researchers have discovered that algae can be raised on these grounds with no other nutrients added. Exposed to twenty hours of light and four hours of darkness each day, the algae eventually yield high-quality biofuel that is renewable and emits very little. These are only a few of the answers nature offers. In return for this second chance, we must keep searching for ways to preserve a healthy, clean environment.

(D) The first concerns plastic waste. Roughly half of all plastic is manufactured for single-use items, which are thrown out after being used only once. So far, under ten percent of the world’s plastic waste has been recycled, and millions of tons drift into the ocean every year, threatening marine species. With this in mind, scientists have turned to mealworms, the larvae of a kind of beetle. Bacteria that inhabit their bodies can decompose plastic, so the worms can literally live on it. Researchers now hope to cultivate these bacteria outside the worms and to accelerate the process by which plastic is broken down.
